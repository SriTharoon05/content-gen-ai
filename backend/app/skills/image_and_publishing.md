# Image composition and publishing rules

Every image is one complete full-frame 9:16 composition in native 1K output. No panels, collage,
blank footer or embedded subtitles. Keep important subjects inside a 10% margin. Final video is
720x1280 at 24 fps. Captions must respect bottom UI safe space.

## Prompt quality (critical — the image model is small)
The renderer is a low-parameter turbo model. Vague prompts produce mush. Every prompt must state,
in this order and in one flowing sentence block:
1. Shot type and lens feel (wide cinematic, medium close-up, low-angle, over-the-shoulder, macro).
2. The single subject and its exact visible action and expression.
3. Wardrobe / age / build / hair — repeated verbatim for a recurring character in every shot.
4. Setting with concrete nouns (wet asphalt road, brass desk lamp, steel gantry).
5. Lighting and time of day (dim sodium streetlight, overcast noon, single candle).
6. Colour grade and atmosphere (dark blue fog, warm amber haze, cold slate tones).
7. Composition and mood keywords (vertical 9:16 framing, dramatic motion blur, shallow depth of field).
Never write text, captions, letters, logos, watermarks or UI into the image. No split screens.
Say "no text" explicitly. Aim for 60–110 words per prompt.

## Continuity
A recurring character's description block must be byte-identical across all their shots. Change only
the action, camera and lighting.

## Publishing
YouTube: specific searchable subject, honest curiosity and a description that delivers context.
Instagram: strong first line, conversational context and at most one meaningful discussion question.
Choose 3–5 topic-specific hashtags, never generic viral spam or invented trend claims. Keep hashtags
in their structured field; the uploader adds them once. No guarantees of reach, engagement bait or
unsupported claims. Fiction uses story tension; factual explainers use an answerable curiosity gap.
Decide made-for-kids from intended audience, not animation style. Publishing visibility and approval
are controlled by operator settings, never by the copywriting agent.
